Introduction

A selector for a quotient \(\mathbb{R}/G\) is a set containing exactly one element from each coset. The most famous instance is the Vitali selector, the case \(G = \mathbb{Q}\): a set picking out one real from each equivalence class of \(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Q}\). No such set can be constructed explicitly — its existence is a weak fragment of the Axiom of Choice, unprovable in ZF alone — and it is the standard example of a nonmeasurable set.

This post is about a question that sounds like it should be easy. Suppose you are handed a selector for \(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Q}\). Can you build one for \(\mathbb{R}/G\), where \(G\) is some other countable dense subgroup of \(\mathbb{R}\) — say \(G = p\mathbb{Z}+q\mathbb{Z}\) with \(p/q\notin\mathbb{Q}\)? And conversely? Intuitively the answer ought to be yes in both directions: these quotients all look alike, and being able to choose from the cosets of one really does feel like it should let you choose from the cosets of another.

It does turn out to be yes. But I do not know of any elementary proof, and the only route I have found runs through the theory of hyperfinite Borel equivalence relations — a corner of descriptive set theory that is still an active research area. This post lays out that route.

I ran into this question while writing Pathological Periodicity, Part III. There I prove that certain versions of the periodic decomposition theorem are equivalent, over ZF, to the existence of a Vitali selector. The proofs of those decomposition theorems, however, do not invoke choice for \(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Q}\); they invoke it for quotients \(\mathbb{R}/G\) with \(G\) of finite rank \(\geq 2\). Closing that gap is exactly what the theorem below does, and justifying it properly turned out to need a post of its own.

A warning before we start: I do not understand the machinery involved at any real depth. What follows is a route through the literature that makes me confident the statement is true, not a proof I could defend line by line. I flag the places where I am least sure.

The statement

Theorem 1 (Selector transfer) Let \(G\) be a countable, dense subgroup of \(\mathbb{R}\). Then a selector for \(\mathbb{R}/G\) exists if and only if a selector for \(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Q}\) exists.

As I said above, the statement is not surprising in itself. What is surprising is how much machinery it seems to take. I haven’t quite seen this theorem stated as-is anywhere; what I am doing here is weaving together results from a variety of papers/preprints, including some published in recent years. In other words, we are touching on some pretty deep facts in descriptive set theory that are part of active areas of research!

Some definitions

Everything below concerns equivalence relations on \(\mathbb{R}\), or more generally on a Polish (separable, completely metrizable) space. The relation we care about is the one induced by a subgroup: for \(G\leq \mathbb{R}\), write \[x \mathrel{\sim_G} y \quad :\Longleftrightarrow\quad x - y \in G,\] so that the equivalence classes of \(\sim_G\) are exactly the cosets of \(G\), and a selector for \(\mathbb{R}/G\) is exactly a transversal for \(\sim_G\) — a set meeting each class exactly once. The case \(G = \mathbb{Q}\) is called the Vitali equivalence relation.

An equivalence relation \(E\) on a Polish space \(X\) is Borel if, viewed as a subset of \(X\times X\), it is a Borel set. It is a countable Borel equivalence relation if in addition every equivalence class is countable. Each \(\sim_G\) with \(G\) countable is a countable Borel equivalence relation: it is the preimage of the Borel set \(G\) under the continuous map \((x,y)\mapsto x-y\), and its classes are the cosets of a countable group.

\(E\) is finite if all of its classes are finite, and hyperfinite if it can be written as an increasing union \[E = \bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}} E_n, \qquad E_0\subseteq E_1\subseteq E_2\subseteq\cdots,\] of finite Borel equivalence relations \(E_n\). Hyperfiniteness is a strong tameness condition, and which countable Borel equivalence relations are hyperfinite is precisely the subject of the papers cited below.

\(E\) is smooth if there is a Borel map \(\theta: X\to Y\) into some Polish space \(Y\) such that \(x \mathrel{E} y\) if and only if \(\theta(x) = \theta(y)\) — informally, if the classes of \(E\) can be told apart by a Borel-definable invariant. It is nonsmooth otherwise. For countable Borel equivalence relations, smoothness is equivalent to the existence of a Borel transversal, and it is this reformulation that we will actually use.

The route through the literature

So here is the route of papers/preprints that makes me fairly confident Theorem 1 holds, even though I do not yet understand the machinery employed at any depth. First, a preprint by Larson and Zapletal working within ZF set theory uses, in the course of their argument, the fact that for any two “hyperfinite and nonsmooth” Borel equivalence relations, there exists a selector for one if there exists a selector for the other.1 Larson-Zapletal cite a classic 1994 paper on hyperfinite Borel equivalence relations by Dougherty, Jackson and Kechris (DJK) for this fact.2 DJK and Larson-Zapletal list the Vitali equivalence relation induced by \(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Q}\) as an example of a hyperfinite nonsmooth relation, so the question becomes whether this holds for the equivalence relation induced by \(\mathbb{R}/G\) for any countable dense subgroup \(G\).

Hyperfiniteness for all such quotients (not just \(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Q}\)) follows from a paper by J. Frisch and F. Shinko.3 I don’t have a direct citation for nonsmoothness, but it follows from the definition of nonsmoothness given by DJK, the density of \(G\), and nonmeasurability of selectors for \(\mathbb{R}/G\); I spell that argument out in the next section. From here, Theorem 1 follows from DJK and Larson-Zapletal.

Nonsmoothness of \(\sim_G\)

If \(G\) is a countable dense subgroup of \(\mathbb{R}\) and \(\sim_G\) is the equivalence relation induced by the quotient \(\mathbb{R}/G\), then any selector for \(\mathbb{R}/G\) is necessarily non-measurable, and thus cannot be Borel. The proof of this is basically the same as the proof of the nonmeasurability of a Vitali set. But, according to DJK, nonsmoothness is equivalent to the nonexistence of a Borel selector for any countable Borel equivalence relation, including \(\sim_G\). Thus \(\sim_G\) is nonsmooth.

Where this leaves things

The chain, then, is: \(\sim_G\) is a countable Borel equivalence relation for every countable \(G\leq\mathbb{R}\); it is hyperfinite by Frisch–Shinko; it is nonsmooth whenever \(G\) is dense, by the argument just given; and DJK’s transfer result, in the ZF formulation used by Larson–Zapletal, moves a selector between any two hyperfinite nonsmooth Borel equivalence relations. Taking one of them to be the Vitali relation gives Theorem 1.
